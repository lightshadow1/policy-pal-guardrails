# GLiNER fine-tune on the four labels (2026-11-04)

## What it tests

Does fine-tuning GLiNER2.5-Decide on week 1 plus the generated questions improve it on real held-out traffic
(week 2)? The scoring check (`data/train/README.md`) showed GLiNER gets about half of the cleanly labelled
generated questions wrong, so the data has something to teach it.

## Setup (my calls)

- **Training:** 1,344 records. 880 from week 1 (an 85% split, stratified by source and label) plus all 464
  generated questions.
- **Dev:** the other 156 week 1 questions, real traffic only. They pick the best checkpoint and tune thresholds.
- **Test:** week 2 is never trained or tuned on. `build` refuses any training text identical to a week 2 text.
- **Schema:** `topic` (the six round 2 topics), `scope` (in scope vs irrelevant), `clarity` (ambiguous vs clear).
  All three are multi-label with the round 2 definitions.
  `scope` is taught only on in-scope and irrelevant rows.
- **Text:** PII-scrubbed, as every guardrail sees it.
- **Comparison:** GLiNER base on the same schema, tuned on the same dev set. Jev round 2 is also tuned on the same
  dev set for reference.
- **Training settings:** `finetune/train.py`. LoRA r=16 on encoder and classifier, LR 5e-4, 3 epochs,
  batch 2 × 4, gradient checkpointing, fp32, early stopping on dev loss. Seeds 42, 43 and 44.

## Results (week 2, `results/finetune/report.md`)

| | thresholds | accuracy | macro-F1 | in-scope answered | in-scope refused | irrelevant | not allowed | ambiguous |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| GLiNER zero-shot | 0.5 | 24.3% | 12.6% | 0% | 99.6% | 0.6% | 93.9% | 9.3% |
| GLiNER zero-shot, tuned on all of week 1 (main report) | tuned | 48.1% | 40.1% | 57.3% | 42.3% | 34.4% | 73.9% | 9.3% |
| GLiNER fine-tuned (3 seeds) | 0.5 | 65.8–66.7% | 40.9–41.1% | 94–95% | 5–6% | 94–96% | **0%** | 0–2% |
| GLiNER fine-tuned (3 seeds) | dev-tuned | 34.2–54.8% | 29.1–48.5% | 39–93% | 7–60% | 2–25% | 76–97% | 0–20% |
| Jev round 2 | dev-tuned | 62.7% | 56.0% | 49.6% | 8.1% | 66.8% | 70.5% | 44.4% |

**What it shows:**

- **The fine-tune moved GLiNER's errors more than it removed them.**
  - It learned in-scope vs off-topic very well: about 94% of in-scope questions answered and about 95% of
    off-topic questions caught at 0.5. Zero-shot GLiNER refused 42% of in-scope questions even after tuning.
  - Its topic scores stay below 0.5, so at the default threshold it refuses nothing (0% of not-allowed). The 66%
    accuracy comes from week 2's mix, not from guarding.
- **Thresholds can't rescue it with this little held-out data.** The dev set has 156 questions, 8 of them
  ambiguous.
  - Tuned thresholds bring not-allowed recall up to 76–97%, but break off-topic detection.
  - Results swing from 34% to 55% accuracy across seeds that differ only in random initialisation and batch order.
  - The same dev set also breaks zero-shot GLiNER (19.1%), so the noise comes from the threshold data, not the
    model.
- **On the balanced measure, fine-tuning lands where tuning did.** Fine-tuned macro-F1 is about 41% at either
  setting, against 40.1% for zero-shot GLiNER tuned on week 1. Using the labelled week 1 data for training instead
  of for thresholds gave about the same overall result, with a different error profile.
- **Jev stays clearly ahead** (macro-F1 56.0% tuned on the same dev set; 63.6% for round 2b in the main report),
  with no training.
- **The cost of the local route, measured:** about 1,350 labelled examples (880 real plus 464 generated), 17 GPU
  minutes per seed, 67 minutes of GPU time, and a result that needs more labelled data for calibration before
  it could guard anything.

## Week 2 check (2026-11-04)

- **Week 2 is clean in the strict sense.** `data/testset.jsonl` is unchanged since the last commit (weeks frozen).
  None of its 1,055 questions is in the fine-tune's train or dev split, by id or by exact text.
- **But there are near-duplicates.** 67 week 2 questions (6%) share 60% or more of their words with a training
  record: 57 CLINC paraphrases, 7 Policy Pal log variants with different fake PII, and 3 deepset prompts. Most are
  in scope. This is the final review's M12 point (the weeks are random halves of one pool). It's harmless for
  zero-shot models, but it partly tests a fine-tune on what it memorised.
- **What the near-duplicates do to the numbers:**

| model | accuracy on the 67 near-duplicates | accuracy on the other 988 | macro-F1 on the other 988 |
|---|---|---|---|
| GLiNER fine-tuned, 3 seeds at 0.5 | 82.1–83.6% | 64.6–65.7% | 40.4–40.7% |
| GLiNER zero-shot, tuned on week 1 | 50.7% | 47.9% | 40.0% |
| Jev round 2, tuned on week 1 | 50.7% | 62.0% | 55.3% |

  The fine-tune gains about 18 points on the near-duplicates, about 1 point overall. On the clean 988, the
  conclusions hold: the fine-tune matches tuned zero-shot GLiNER on macro-F1, and Jev is well ahead.
- **For any future fine-tune:** split by paraphrase group, not by row. Keep every CLINC intent, and every Policy Pal
  question with its PII variants, on one side of the split.

## Calibration and ranking (week 2, diagnostic only; no threshold was chosen from it)

| model | not-allowed ROC-AUC (max topic score) | median topic score: not allowed / others | ECE | scope ROC-AUC (in scope vs irrelevant) | ambiguous ROC-AUC |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| fine-tuned s42 | 0.740 | 0.000 / 0.000 | 0.250 | 0.986 | 0.461 |
| fine-tuned s43 | 0.715 | 0.000 / 0.000 | 0.250 | 0.986 | 0.602 |
| fine-tuned s44 | 0.450 | 0.000 / 0.000 | 0.250 | 0.990 | 0.738 |
| GLiNER zero-shot | 0.513 | 0.758 / 0.757 | 0.499 | 0.764 | 0.459 |
| Jev | 0.906 | 0.850 / 0.060 | 0.030 | 0.980 | 0.715 |

Per-topic AUC (that topic vs rows that aren't not_allowed):
- fine-tune: 0.51–0.94;
- zero-shot: 0.80–0.96;
- Jev: 0.96–0.99.

**Reading:**
- **The fine-tune's topic part collapsed to near-zero outputs; it isn't just miscalibrated.** About 28% of training
  rows had a topic, with each topic positive in 3–10% of rows, and early stopping on dev loss rewarded "no topic".
  Ranking is weak (AUC 0.45–0.74), so thresholds can't fully recover it.
- **Zero-shot GLiNER is miscalibrated but ranks within each topic.** All topics sit around 0.76, so it needs
  per-topic thresholds.
- **The fine-tune's scope part is the real gain:** AUC 0.986–0.990, above Jev (0.980) and zero-shot (0.764).
- **Jev is well-ranked and well-calibrated** (ECE 0.03).

**Fixes for a future fine-tune:**
- a single-choice topic task with an explicit "none" label;
- oversample not_allowed rows or weight positive labels;
- pick the checkpoint by macro-F1, not dev loss;
- split by paraphrase group.

A hybrid (the fine-tuned scope part plus Jev for topics) is also worth testing.

## Version 2: fixing the recipe (2026-11-04)

| v1 problem | v2 change |
|---|---|
| Topic part collapsed to "no topic" | `topic` and `clarity` are single-choice; `topic` gains an explicit `none` label. `scope` stays multi-label (AUC 0.99 in v1). |
| Sparse topic examples | Oversample in training: not_allowed ×2, ambiguous ×3. |
| Checkpoint chosen by dev loss | Score both `best` (dev loss) and `final`; `evaluate` keeps the one with higher dev macro-F1 at 0.5. |
| Dev too small (156 questions, 8 ambiguous) | Dev is 25% of week 1: 239 questions. |
| 67 week 2 near-duplicates in training | Drop week 1 rows sharing ≥60% of words with any week 2 question: 74 dropped from train and dev. |

- **Data:** `data/finetune_v2/`. 1,726 training records (723 week 1 + 464 generated before oversampling), 239 dev.
  v1's data and results stay as committed; rebuild them with `build --version 1`.
- **Code changes:**
  - `ppguard/finetune.py` gains per-version configs (5 new tests, suite 86);
  - `finetune/predict_tasks.py` reads single-choice outputs, as well as multi-label ones;
  - `run_all.sh` scores both checkpoints at batch size 4;
  - `remote.sh` takes `FT_VERSION=2`.
- **Run:** v1 outputs on the box moved to `out_v1/`. The chain started at 22:33 EST.

### Version 2 results (2026-11-05, `results/finetune_v2/report.md`)

- **Run:** the chain finished at 00:05 EST (`ALL_DONE`). Each seed trained in about 22 min, peak 2.75 GiB.
- **Training data was correct:** 1,036 `none` and 690 topic targets, all single-choice, all labels valid.
- **Checkpoints picked by dev macro-F1 at 0.5:** s42 best, s43 final, s44 best.

| week 2 | thresholds | accuracy | macro-F1 | in-scope answered | irrelevant | not allowed | ambiguous |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| fine-tune v2 (3 seeds) | 0.5 | 65.6–67.4% | 40.0–43.0% | 95–97% | 93–97% | **0%** | 0–4% |
| fine-tune v2 (3 seeds) | dev-tuned | 25.0% | 10.0% | 0% | 0% | 100% | 0% |
| GLiNER zero-shot | dev-tuned | 51.7% | 43.2% | 58.5% | 47.3% | 62.1% | 11.1% |
| Jev round 2 | dev-tuned | 75.5% | 62.8% | 83.8% | 79.5% | 72.7% | 14.8% |

**What v2 shows:**
- **The topic part collapsed again.** All three seeds choose `none` for all 1,055 week 2 questions, with
  confidence 1.0. Clarity collapsed to `clear` too. Only the balanced scope decision is learned.
- **Tuned thresholds can't help:** all topic scores are 0, so tuning picks 0 and refuses everything.
- **Across both recipes:** with about 1,200 examples and LoRA r16, GLiNER learns the balanced binary decision and
  falls back to the majority class elsewhere. Oversampling 2–3× and a forced choice didn't change that.
- **Recommendation:** stop. The next options are bigger redesigns, each another GPU run: one balanced binary task
  per topic, a class-weighted loss, or much more topic data.
- **Caveat for the main report.** Jev round 2's tuned week 2 accuracy is 61.3% with thresholds tuned on all of
  week 1, and 75.5% tuned on this 239-question dev set. The difference is mostly the ambiguous threshold (the round
  2b issue). Thresholds tuned on small labelled sets are unstable, so quote the tuned numbers with that caveat.
