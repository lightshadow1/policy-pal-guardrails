# Policy Pal guardrails: project summary (2026-10-29)

A note to come back to. It covers what was built, what it found, what you decided, what I decided for you, and
what's still open.

## In one paragraph

We built a 2,091-question test set with four labels: in scope, irrelevant, not allowed (with a topic) and
ambiguous. It's split into two "weeks of traffic". We measured three guardrails on it: Policy Pal's regex as
shipped, Jev, and GLiNER base zero-shot. Round 1 used day-one topics; round 2 added what week 1 showed. The
argument: blocked topics are found in traffic, not
specified up front; the guardrail must be cheap to change; and privacy decides which option you may use.

## Takeaways

1. **The shipped guardrail only works on its author's questions.** It's 90.5% right on Policy Pal's own dev
   questions and 26.5% right on realistic traffic. It catches 4.5% of not-allowed requests and 0% of privacy ones,
   and it answers 92% of off-topic questions.
2. **The topics that mattered weren't the day-one ones.** Real traffic was dominated by off-topic chatter, privacy
   and harmful requests. Privacy, the biggest gap, wasn't on the list.
3. **Fixing it is cheap with Jev.** Four plain-English questions took it from 33% to 53% with no labelled data, and
   to 61% once thresholds were tuned on about 1,000 labelled week 1 questions.
4. **The local option needs labelled data and still over-refuses.** GLiNER stays at 24% untuned. Tuned, it reaches
   48% but refuses about 4 in 10 legitimate questions.
5. **Measure "answered", not just "refused".** In round 2, Jev's refusal rate looked fine at 6.4%. Meanwhile its
   ambiguity check asked for detail on 110 of the 234 in-scope questions, so only 47% got answered. Checking in
   scope before ambiguous (round 2b, post hoc) raised that to 88%. A dashboard that counts only blocks would have
   shown nothing wrong.
6. **Privacy decides the path.** If HR traffic may go to an API under a DPA, with personal data stripped first, the
   route is Jev plus a weekly review of the traffic. If not, the local model needs the labelled week and still
   over-refuses. Either way, budget for labelling a week of traffic.

## Headline results (week 2, 1,055 held-out questions)

The week 2 mix is 234 in scope, 503 off-topic, 264 not allowed and 54 ambiguous. Half are off-topic, so compare
guardrails rather than reading any single number as "what you'd get".

| Guardrail | Accuracy (95% CI) | In-scope answered | In-scope refused | Labelled data used |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Regex, as shipped | 26.5% (23.9–29.2) | 98.7% | 1.3% | none |
| Jev, round 1 (day one) | 33.1% (30.1–35.8) | 95.7% | 4.3% | none |
| Jev, round 2, threshold 0.5 | 53.3% (50.3–56.1) | 24.8% | 5.1% | none |
| Jev, round 2, tuned | 61.3% (58.4–64.1) | 46.6% | 6.4% | ~1,000 week 1 questions |
| Jev, round 2b, threshold 0.5 *(post hoc)* | 66.4% (63.6–69.3) | 86.3% | 5.1% | none |
| Jev, round 2b, tuned *(post hoc)* | 70.3% (67.4–73.0) | 88.0% | 6.4% | ~1,000 week 1 questions |
| GLiNER, round 1 | 21.6% (19.1–24.1) | 0.4% | 99.6% | none |
| GLiNER, round 2, threshold 0.5 | 24.3% (21.7–26.8) | 0.0% | 99.6% | none |
| GLiNER, round 2, tuned | 48.1% (44.9–51.1) | 57.3% | 42.3% | ~1,000 week 1 questions |

Things worth remembering:
- **Regex answers almost everything.** It answers 92% of off-topic questions, too. It's 90.5% right on Policy Pal's
  own dev questions and 0% on the law, health and Ask Ubuntu excerpts.
- **Privacy was the topic nobody wrote down.** Jev went from catching 0% of privacy requests to 94.4%. Harmful
  requests went from 8.9% to 73.3%.
- **Untuned GLiNER scores are inverted.** In-scope questions get *higher* top-topic scores than not-allowed ones
  (median 0.70 vs 0.62). The sick-day question scores 0.74 for medical advice, against Jev's 0.05.
- **Round 2b checks in scope before ambiguous.** It exists because Jev's ambiguity check asked for detail on 110 of
  the 234 in-scope questions. I first noticed this in week 2 results, then confirmed it on week 1 (99 of 230). So
  2b isn't a clean held-out result, and it's labelled post hoc.
- **What adding the discovered topics cost:**
  - Jev: 4 questions, 92 words.
  - GLiNER: 6 descriptions, 114 words.
  - No retraining for either. The *tuned* numbers used about 1,000 labelled week 1 questions; the 0.5 numbers
    used none.
- **Cost and speed:** Jev costs $25.70 per million questions in round 2, with a p50 latency of 94 ms. GLiNER is
  self-hosted, with a p50 of 80 ms. All Jev runs together cost about $0.10.

## The labels and who decided them

**Where the 2,091 labels came from:**
- 1,370 came from the source datasets. 137 of those were spot-checked, and 24 corrected.
- Claude drafted the other 721.

**The Opus review** covered all 858 drafts and spot-checks: 740 agree, 28 change, 90 unsure.

**Your decisions on the 90 unsure rows:**
- **CLINC 401k rollover (30 rows):** not allowed, investment. Both reviewers would have said in scope with a
  redirect to HR.
- **CLINC taxes (29 rows):** irrelevant.
- **General health with no personal case (7 rows):** irrelevant.
- **The other 24:** you accepted Opus's best guesses.
- **Rule sweep:** you then said to apply all 42 rows that your investment and medical rules affect elsewhere
  (`data/review/rule_sweep.csv`).

**A second reviewer**, GPT-6, ran blind from `data/review/external/PROMPT.md`:
- it agreed with Opus on 84% of rows (κ 0.78), and with the final labels on 79% (κ 0.72);
- all 54 rows where both reviewers disagree with the final label are your policy calls;
- re-scoring with GPT-6's labels moves Jev round 2b from 70.3% to 68.2%, and the ranking doesn't change
  (`results/review/reviewer_agreement.md`).

**The rules** are written in `data/labelling_guide.md`, under "Decisions after review":
- investment means advice on a decision; a lookup is irrelevant;
- 401k rollovers are investment;
- medical needs a personal case;
- self-harm disclosures are harmful;
- "next holiday" questions are covered=no.

## Choices I made for you (check these)

1. **No worktree or per-task commits.** The repo had no commits, and you commit only when you ask. *If wrong:*
   nothing; the history is one commit.
2. **Round 1 thresholds are 0.5.** Day one has no traffic to tune on. A threshold that week 1 can't fit, because it
   has no positives or no negatives, also stays at 0.5. *If wrong:* one threshold left untuned.
3. **Round 2 keeps round 1's wording word for word,** even though you refined the investment and medical rules
   during the review. Those refinements came from rows in both weeks, so putting them into the definitions would
   leak week 2. *If wrong:* some investment and medical lookups still get flagged in round 2.
4. **In round 1, Jev and GLiNER replace only the regex checks.** Policy Pal's keyword retrieval still decides in
   scope versus irrelevant, as it does in the shipped pipeline. *If wrong:* round 1 understates what the models
   could do on day one.
5. **CLINC's PTO-balance and status questions are covered=no,** and so is "next holiday": those are personal
   records, and §1.4 lists no dates. *If wrong:* about 120 rows move between the covered and not-covered
   false-refusal splits.
6. **Pair 10 was reworded,** because it duplicated a real Policy Pal log question. *If wrong:* nothing.
7. **The 50/50 week split** is stratified by source and label, with pairs kept together, and weeks are now frozen
   per id. *If wrong:* at most one row per stratum differs.
8. **Smaller ones:**
   - StackExchange notes keep the question id, for CC-BY-SA attribution;
   - `labelled_by` compares the full label, topic and covered combination;
   - doubts about spot-check labels went in `draft_reason`, not `draft_label`;
   - the report skips a model whose cache is incomplete, rather than crashing.

## Re-running

```
uv run pytest                                          # offline, no credentials
uv run python -m ppguard.build assemble                # rebuild the test set from labels.csv (weeks frozen)
uv run python -m ppguard.runner --guard jev --round 2  # needs .env (TYPESAFE_API_KEY); cached rows are free
uv run python -m ppguard.runner --guard gliner --round 2   # needs the GLiNER service on the GPU box
uv run python -m ppguard.report --round 2              # writes results/report.md
```
